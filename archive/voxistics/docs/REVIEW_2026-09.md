## 1. Performance, frame delivery and architecture

### What a frame costs
From your F3 screenshot (Release, 1080p, idle in a small build):
- CPU **work**: 1.05 ms average.
- **Present**: about 15.6 ms, which is almost all waiting for vsync.
- The CPU is not the limit. The worst CPU spike was **MUSIC at 7.68 ms**.

The GPU was never measured. **Done:** F3 and the Ctrl+F3 report now show **GPU SHADOW / WORLD / POST / UI**, from timestamp queries read back three frames late, so measuring never stalls anything. That's the number to watch on your machine.

Most of the GPU time is probably the **world pixel shader**. Every pixel of every block does all of this:
- two colour texture reads (sharp and smooth, blended with distance) plus one surface-map read;
- three value-noise evaluations (the large-scale colour variation);
- a surface frame built from screen-space derivatives (normal mapping);
- four shadow-map taps;
- a 3D glow-grid read;
- the fog and atmosphere colour.

(The glow-grid read is already skipped when no glowing block is near.)

If the GPU rows show the world pass near 16 ms, two cheap cuts are available:
1. Take the smooth texture read only where it's actually blended in (beyond 10 blocks). Close up, that saves one read per pixel.
2. Replace the three noise evaluations with one read from a small pre-baked tileable noise texture.

Each keeps the look and takes minutes. I haven't done them yet because the GPU numbers should decide.

### Frame reliability: fixed
- **Done — music off the main thread.** A worker thread synthesizes the music. The frame never pays for it, including the four chunks that used to be generated at once on every resume (about 5 ms each time you unpaused, more in busy sections).
- **Done — catch-up cap.** After a stall, at most five simulation ticks run in one frame. Before, up to 15 could run back to back, which made the next frame slow too: a hitch that fed itself.
- **Done — pulse routing** no longer runs a search with a fresh hash map for every pulse. Each network keeps per-outlet step tables, so boosted harvester farms sending hundreds of pulses a second stay cheap.

### Frame reliability: proposals
1. **Vsync + the frame cap can judder on displays that aren't 60 Hz.** With vsync on and a cap that isn't a whole divisor of the refresh rate (60 on a 75 or 144 Hz screen), frames alternate between one and two refreshes. Fix without reading the display: with vsync on, don't apply the cap (vsync paces), and apply it only with vsync off. On your 60 Hz setup nothing changes.
2. **Saves write on the main thread.** Autosave encodes and writes the whole save in one frame. Small today; with big builds it becomes a hitch every autosave. Fix: encode on the main thread (it's fast) and write the file on a background thread.
3. **Terrain generation and chunk meshing are main-thread**, budgeted at 6 meshes a frame. That's fine for now. A worker thread for meshing is the next step if walking into new ground ever stutters.

### Memory: no fatal flaws found
- No leaks in the steady state. GPU buffers are released on rebuild and resize. A few are left for the OS to reclaim at exit, which is normal.
- Mesh building, meshing queues, the profiler and now the UI (**done**) reuse their buffers; nothing allocates per frame in the hot paths.
- **Done:** The Line's per-cell history no longer grows with everywhere you've ever walked. Cells faded below a second are dropped when saving.
- Modified chunks you walk away from are kept in memory (and in the save). That scales with what you've changed, not with distance, which is the design.
- One pointer handoff was worth checking: the music pool can be abandoned if the audio device never drains. It leaks rather than risking a use-after-free, deliberately, and the worker thread respects that.

### Architecture and organisation
Following your new "organisation and careful annotation" priority:
- **game.cpp is ~1,900 lines doing seven jobs**: input, menus, HUD, overlays, the store view, the tutorial and the save flows. Proposal: split it into `input.cpp`, `menus.cpp`, `hud.cpp` (hotbar, F3, F7, toasts, tutorial) and `storeview.cpp`, with no behaviour change. It's the biggest readability win available.
- **render.cpp (~2,150 lines)** carries its HLSL as C strings. That keeps the exe self-contained and `check_shaders.py` checks every variant, so I'd keep it, but move the strings to a `shaders.h`.
- **sfx_synth.cpp** is large but cohesive (one system); leave it.
- The seed files (cc_2_2_2.cpp and the others) aren't compiled into the game. Good.

## 2. How the music fits the game

**What works.**
- One clock drives the sky, the light, the music and the world's sounds, so sunrise *is* the music's dawn.
- The soundscape colours the track (positive / active / mechanical), and every world sound is harmony-locked to it. That's rare, and it makes the game's audio feel of a piece.
- Pausing is silence, which lands as "time stopped".

**What doesn't yet.**
- **Repetition.** It's the same hour every hour. In a long session you'll hear the same section at the same time of day, every day. You already called it "boring, like the evenly spaced horn". The fix that fits the design is seeded variation per day. The form stays (sections locked to the clock), while each day draws its own motif ordering, voicings, and which layers enter when, from the day number. It stays deterministic, costs no CPU, and no two days are alike.
- **No breathing room.** The track rarely drops to just its bed. Stretches of near-silence (especially midday and deep night) make the music's return mean something, and would let the world's sounds carry more of the day.
- **The factory has no voice yet.** Harvesters, pipes, stores and diffusers make no sound. Proposals, all through the existing palette (harmony-locked, flash-free by nature):
  - a soft tick per gathered pulse, quantised to the beat;
  - a breathy exhale from diffusers;
  - a short note on a catch, pitched by the pulse's spin (plain, clockwise and anticlockwise each a chord tone).

  A busy factory would then *sound* busy, and in tune.
- **The Line and the music.** The sky already races near the line. Letting the music's playback time lean slightly ahead there too (at most a few percent, re-anchoring as you leave) would make time-running-differently audible as well as visible. That's a bigger decision; your call.

**Recommendation order:** the factory's sounds first (they're missing), then per-day variation (it fixes the repetition), then breathing room.

## 3. The texture library: a direction scoped to the project

**Where it stands.** 46 natural textures, 10 industry textures, and about 14 older procedural placeholders (foundation, chest, machine, the old tube, glass, crystal and a few more). You've found much of the natural set unconvincing, and the industry set is the first one built to a single brief.

**Proposed direction: four material families, each with its own brief, and nothing outside them.**
1. **Land.** Ground, stone, water, plants.
   - Muted, earthy, low saturation.
   - Relief through tufts, cracks and strata rather than colour speckle.
   - No tiny repeated details (the grass flowers were the lesson).
2. **Industry.** Machines, pipes, storage.
   - Sage enamel, pewter and zinc, as now.
   - The rule that made it work: materials stay quiet, and saturated colour is reserved for meaning.
3. **The strange layer.** The Line, essence, pulse.
   - Its own visual language: light and glow rather than pigment.
   - Owns the three pulse colours: yellow-amber, blue, red.
   - Nothing else in the world may use those hues at full strength, so they always read as "pulse".
4. **The threat.** Ooze rifts (Part XX).
   - The existing flesh set (veined flesh, sores, membrane) is the seed.
   - Wet, dark, faintly glowing.
   - Kept for when the threat is built.

**Rules across all four:**
- The palette rule above keeps meaning readable, and makes colour-vision modes simpler: remap three hues, not a whole library.
- **Value range per family:** land mid-tone, industry darker, strange layer bright, so shapes read apart at a glance.
- Replace the old procedural placeholders family by family. The chest and machine, which the pulse chain uses, go first.
- Every new texture goes through a generator script (as industry does now), so tweaks are one parameter, not a redraw.
- A **contact sheet** tool renders every texture on a block, a slab and a pipe, in daylight and dusk, on one page, so you can judge the pile at once instead of block by block in the library.

**How that shapes the big batch you asked for:** generate it family by family against these briefs, lay it out as contact sheets, and you pick. Rejects never reach the library.
